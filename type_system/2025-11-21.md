#### type variables vs. type variables

https://github.com/ocaml/ocaml/issues/14253

What does `type a` actually mean, why is not just a rigid polymorphic variable?

Jacques: Alistair was pointing that you can give a reasonable meaning to that. Whether you can implement it easily is another question.

Gabriel: what I would like in the long term is,
- `'a` becomes truly polymorphic, we write `'_a` for the flexible inference variable
- over time `type a` and `'a` become synonymous

Gabriel: if we allow `let f (type a)` ... as System F abstraction, people are going to ask for applications/instantiations at the callsite as well.

Jacques: I should have a look at whether we can implement this.

Gabriel: naively, I would think that you strengthen approximation by approximating polymorphism first.

Jacques: but then it matters that we do catch all occurrences of `a` in the annotations in the arguments, not just the one at the toplevel. Maybe we can guarantee this for irrefutable patterns, but I'm not sure. Missing some occurrences of `a` in the approximation could prove very confusing.

Richard: instead of trying to make this polymorphic recursive, we could ...

Gabriel: I think this correponds to the proposal of Florian to think of `let rec f (type a) ...` as `fun (type a) -> let rec f ...`.

But note that if we introduce `type a` because there is GADT matching inside `f`, then probably we in fact do want polymorphic recursion.

Note that this discussion becomes less important with `val choose` allowed.

Gabriel tries to gather consensus on the fact that Jacques volunteered to look at this again, see what is possible to implement. (polymorphic recursion or not)

Samuel: with Leo I looked at approximating under `fun (type a) -> ...`. If we try to approximate below it, it is tricky, because you have to decide whether something that shows up is at the level of `a` or one level below.
